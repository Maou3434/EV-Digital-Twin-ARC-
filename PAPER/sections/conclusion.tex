\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework for predicting dynamic cell temperatures in electric vehicle battery systems. By embedding the lumped-parameter thermal energy balance equation as a soft constraint in the loss function and constraining the learned convective heat transfer coefficient ($hA$) to positive values via a softplus function, the model preserves physical consistency. We addressed the exposure bias problem in autoregressive rollouts by injecting Gaussian noise into the input temperature during training. 

When evaluated on an unseen WLTP2 drive cycle, the PINN outperformed the baseline MLP, reducing the maximum rollout error from 0.2474$^\circ$C to 0.0851$^\circ$C. The learned convective heat transfer coefficient converged to a physically consistent value ($\approx 36.49$ W/K). The results demonstrate that combining physics constraints with noise injection provides a robust, fast, and physically consistent thermal digital twin suitable for real-time battery management systems.
