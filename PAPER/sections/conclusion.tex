\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) digital twin framework for dynamic battery cell temperature prediction. The approach addresses key challenges in real-time thermal modeling by combining lumped-parameter ODE loss regularization, input noise injection to mitigate exposure bias, and softplus-constrained physical parameter estimation. Multi-step autoregressive rollout evaluation across unseen drive cycles confirmed that perturbing input states during training reduces long-term trajectory drift while softplus parameterization ensures physically valid heat transfer parameter estimation.

Rather than relying on purely black-box learning, embedding physical domain knowledge provides soft regularization that helps maintain physical consistency under dynamic load transients. This makes the hybrid framework applicable to real-time battery digital twins, thermal state estimation, and cooling control in electric vehicle battery management systems. Future research will focus on experimental climate chamber validation, multi-node thermal formulations, online parameter adaptation, and embedded microcontroller deployment.
