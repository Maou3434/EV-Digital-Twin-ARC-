\begin{abstract}
Accurate real-time thermal estimation in lithium-ion battery cells is essential for the safety and efficiency of electric vehicles (EVs). Traditional thermal modeling approaches rely either on computationally intensive finite element methods that cannot run in real time, or on simplified lumped-parameter models that fail to capture complex transients. While machine learning (ML) models offer fast inference, they suffer from exposure bias, where small single-step errors accumulate during autoregressive rollout, causing long-term prediction drift. This paper presents a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework for dynamic battery cell temperature prediction that overcomes these limitations. By integrating the governing lumped-parameter thermal differential equation directly into the neural network's loss function, physical consistency is enforced during training. Furthermore, we address exposure bias through input noise injection, forcing the model to learn error-correcting trajectories. The PINN incorporates a trainable convective heat transfer parameter ($hA$) constrained to be positive via a softplus activation, avoiding physically impossible negative estimates while naturally accommodating core-to-casing thermal delays. Evaluated on a large dataset of 78 million rows representing varied drive cycles, the proposed model reduces the maximum rollout error on unseen cycle testing (WLTP2) from 0.2474$^\circ$C in the baseline MLP to 0.0851$^\circ$C, while achieving an autoregressive rollout Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.0117$^\circ$C. The learned physical parameters converge to values consistent with experimental setups, proving the utility of this model as a robust thermal digital twin.
\end{abstract}
