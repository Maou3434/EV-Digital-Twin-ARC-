\section{Results}

\subsection{Quantitative Performance Comparison}
The baseline MLP and PINN models were evaluated using both single-step-ahead prediction on test data and multi-step autoregressive rollout across all 87 unseen WLTP2 drive cycles. Table \ref{tab:comparison} presents the comparative evaluation metrics.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Model Performance Comparison}
\label{tab:comparison}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Evaluation Metric & Baseline MLP & Physics-Informed NN \\
\midrule
Single-step MAE ($^\circ$C) & $4.8978 \times 10^{-7}$ & $3.3783 \times 10^{-7}$ \\
Single-step RMSE ($^\circ$C) & $8.6218 \times 10^{-7}$ & $8.9419 \times 10^{-7}$ \\
Rollout MAE ($^\circ$C) & $0.0170$ & $0.0117$ \\
Rollout RMSE ($^\circ$C) & $0.0211$ & $0.0143$ \\
Maximum Rollout Error ($^\circ$C) & $0.2474$ & $0.0851$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Both models achieve low single-step-ahead prediction errors. At $dt = 0.01$~s, step-to-step temperature changes are small, enabling an identity mapping ($\Delta T \approx 0$) to achieve low single-step loss while masking trajectory drift.

The performance differences become apparent during multi-step autoregressive rollout. The PINN achieves lower rollout error across all metrics, reducing rollout MAE by 31.2\% (from 0.0170$^\circ$C to 0.0117$^\circ$C) and maximum rollout error by 65.6\% (from 0.2474$^\circ$C to 0.0851$^\circ$C). This improvement indicates that combining physics regularization with input noise injection improves trajectory stability.

\subsection{Physical Parameter Learning}
During PINN training, the convective heat transfer coefficient ($hA$) is optimized alongside network weights. Starting from an initial value of $hA_{\text{init}} = 0.1$~W/K, the softplus-constrained parameter increases progressively across epochs to its final value in \eqref{eq:learned_ha}:
\begin{equation}
hA_{\text{final}} \approx 36.49 \text{ W/K}
\label{eq:learned_ha}
\end{equation}
As logged in the training history, $hA$ rises from 0.855~W/K at epoch 1 to 36.490~W/K at early stopping (epoch 18). Because the target data represents casing surface temperature from a dual-node Simulink model, this estimated value corresponds to an \textbf{effective convective heat transfer parameter} consistent with the assumed single-node thermal model and surface cooling dynamics.

\subsection{Computational Performance and Efficiency}
To assess real-time execution suitability, performance metrics recorded during training and rollout evaluation are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DataLoader Throughput:} Benchmarked at 3,891.03 samples/second during dataset loading setup (\texttt{num\_workers=4}, \texttt{prefetch\_factor=2}).
    \item \textbf{Training Throughput:} Training execution reached 209,361 to 254,599 samples/second on an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU.
    \item \textbf{Training Duration:} Early stopping halted training at epoch 18, requiring 259 to 317 seconds per epoch (total training time of approximately 1.4 hours).
    \item \textbf{Memory Footprint:} Under PyTorch AMP (fp16) and TF32 execution, peak GPU memory allocation reached 21.3~MB (out of 8~GB VRAM).
    \item \textbf{Rollout Evaluation Speed:} Autoregressive multi-step rollout achieved an average throughput of 2,100 to 2,200 evaluation steps/second per test cycle file, evaluating each 150,000-step dynamic drive cycle in approximately 68 to 84 seconds.
\end{itemize}
